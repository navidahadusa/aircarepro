Q: Why does my furnace blow cold air?
A: The most common causes are a failed ignition sequence (igniter, flame sensor or inducer), a tripped limit switch from poor airflow, a frozen condensate line on a high-efficiency furnace, or a thermostat set to Fan On instead of Auto.
Q: Should I repair or replace my furnace?
A: Repair usually makes sense when a furnace is under about 12–15 years old and one part has failed. Replacement deserves a look when it is 15–20+ years old, the heat exchanger is cracked, or repairs keep adding up. We show you what we found, and estimates on new systems are free.
Q: Can I reset my furnace myself?
A: You can check the thermostat, the furnace power switch, the breaker and the filter. Don’t reset rollout switches, bypass safety switches or open the gas valve yourself; those trips mean something needs a technician’s diagnosis.

## Refrigerant Leak Repair
An air conditioner or heat pump that needs refrigerant has a leak. Chicago Aircare Pro finds the leak and fixes it instead of recharging the system every summer. Topping off without finding the leak just wastes money. Our EPA 608-certified technicians locate the leak, repair it when it’s repairable, and recharge to the manufacturer’s specs.
Signs a customer needs this: The AC runs long but the air isn’t cold enough; Ice on the indoor coil or the larger copper line; A hissing or bubbling sound near the coil or line set; The system was “recharged” last year and is low again
What's included: Low charge confirmed by superheat and subcooling; Electronic, bubble and nitrogen leak testing; Repair or replacement options with prices; Evacuation and recharge to spec; EPA 608-compliant refrigerant handling
Q: Why not just add refrigerant?
A: The leak continues, the system runs inefficiently and risks compressor damage, and refrigerant escapes into the atmosphere. Fixing the leak is the lasting repair.
Q: Is a refrigerant leak dangerous?
A: Common residential refrigerants are not toxic in the small amounts from a typical leak, but in high concentrations they displace oxygen. If you smell a strong chemical odor indoors, ventilate and call.
Q: Is it worth fixing a leak on an old AC?
A: It depends on the leak location, the refrigerant and the system’s age. For an older R-22 system, we show repair and replacement side by side so you can decide.
Q: How do you find a refrigerant leak?
A: We confirm low charge with superheat and subcooling readings, check the common leak points, and use an electronic detector and bubble solution. Hard-to-find leaks are isolated and pressure-tested with nitrogen.
Q: Are your technicians certified to handle refrigerant?
A: Yes. Our technicians are EPA 608 certified for refrigerant handling.

## Whole-Home Humidifiers
A whole-home humidifier mounts on your furnace and adds moisture to the air throughout the house during the heating season. Chicago winter air gets very dry indoors once the heat runs, causing static shocks, dry skin and sinuses, and gaps in wood floors and trim. Chicago Aircare Pro installs and services whole-home humidifiers, sized and controlled so humidity stays comfortable without fogging the windows.
Signs a customer needs this: Static shocks and dry skin all winter; Gaps in hardwood floors or cracking trim; Running several portable humidifiers; Dry sinuses or scratchy throat when the heat runs
What's included: Bypass, fan-powered or steam options compared; Water line and drain connection; Wiring to the furnace and a humidistat or compatible thermostat; Outdoor-temperature compensation setup; Yearly maintenance walkthrough
Q: Is a whole-home humidifier better than portable units?
A: It treats the whole house with far less upkeep, and there are no tanks to refill. Portable units still make sense for a single room or homes without ductwork.
Q: What humidity should my house be in winter?
A: A common winter target is around 30–40% indoors, lower during deep cold to avoid condensation on windows. An outdoor sensor adjusts it automatically.
Q: How often does a humidifier need service?
A: Replace the water panel once a season (more often with hard water) and check the drain and valve. We can do it during your furnace tune-up.
Q: Will a whole-home humidifier work with a boiler?
A: Furnace-mounted humidifiers need ductwork. In boiler homes, a ducted steam humidifier can sometimes be added; otherwise room units are the practical option.

A whole-home dehumidifier removes moisture from the entire house or basement, independently of the air conditioner. Chicago summers are humid, and basements stay damp even when the AC runs, which is a recipe for musty air and mold. Chicago Aircare Pro installs and services ducted and standalone whole-home dehumidifiers that drain continuously, so there are no buckets to empty.
Signs a customer needs this: A musty smell in the basement or closets; Indoor humidity above about 50–60% in summer; Condensation on cold water pipes, ducts or windows; Portable dehumidifiers that fill up every day, or run constantly; The house feels cool but clammy with the AC on
What's included: Sizing to the space and moisture load; Ducted or standalone installation; Dedicated drain to a floor drain or condensate pump; Dehumidistat control and setup
Q: What humidity should my house be in summer?
A: Roughly 40–50% relative humidity in summer is comfortable and discourages mold and dust mites.
Q: Will a dehumidifier fix water coming into my basement?
A: No. Liquid water from foundation cracks or grading needs to be fixed at the source. A dehumidifier controls moisture in the air.
Q: Why is my house clammy even with the AC on?
A: An oversized AC can reach the setpoint and shut off before removing much moisture, and on mild, damp days it may barely run. A dehumidifier works whenever humidity is high.
Q: Do I have to empty a whole-home dehumidifier?
A: No. It drains continuously to a floor drain or condensate pump.

## Air Purifiers
A whole-home air purifier filters the air every time your furnace or AC blower runs, catching dust, pollen, pet dander and fine particles throughout the house. Your HVAC system moves all the air in your home, so it’s the best place to clean it. Chicago Aircare Pro installs media filter cabinets and whole-home air cleaners on forced-air systems, sized so better filtration doesn’t choke your airflow.
Signs a customer needs this: Allergies or asthma symptoms at home; Dust returns quickly after cleaning; Pets in the home; A 1-inch filter that clogs fast or bows in the slot
What's included: Blower capacity and static pressure check; Filter cabinet or air cleaner sizing and install; Sealed at the return to prevent bypass; Filter schedule setup
Q: Will a whole-home air purifier help with allergies?
A: Better filtration reduces airborne allergens like pollen, dust and dander. It works best combined with sealed return ducts and running the fan enough to circulate air.
Q: Will a thicker filter hurt my system?
A: Not if the cabinet and ductwork are sized for it. We measure static pressure and check airflow before and after.
Q: Do you install ozone-producing air cleaners?
A: No. Ozone is a lung irritant, and we don’t recommend devices designed to produce it.
Q: How often do media filters need changing?
A: A 4–5 inch media filter is typically replaced every 6–12 months, depending on the home, pets and how much the system runs.

## UV Air Sanitizers
A UV air sanitizer is a UV-C lamp installed inside your HVAC system, usually aimed at the AC coil and drain pan, to stop mold and bacteria from growing on these damp surfaces. A clean coil keeps airflow and efficiency up and odors down. Chicago Aircare Pro installs and services ozone-free UV systems in Chicago homes, and we’ll tell you plainly whether your home would benefit.
Signs a customer needs this: A musty smell when the AC starts; Slime in the drain pan or a drain that keeps clogging; A dirty indoor coil that keeps coming back; Household members with allergies
What's included: Coil or air-stream UV options explained; Lamp mounted near the coil, with a safety switch where required; Power connection, often from the furnace; Labelled access panel and lamp replacement schedule
Q: Are UV lights worth it?
A: They’re most useful for keeping coils and drain pans clean in humid conditions. If your home doesn’t have musty smells, drain slime or recurring coil growth, we’ll tell you plainly it probably isn’t worth adding.
Q: Is UV-C safe?
A: The lamp is enclosed inside the HVAC system. UV-C should never be looked at directly. Turn the power off before opening the plenum, and we label the access panel.
Q: Does UV produce ozone?
A: We install lamps rated as ozone-free for residential HVAC.
Q: How often does a UV lamp need replacing?
A: Typically about once a year. We show you how and when during installation.

## Ductwork Repair
Chicago Aircare Pro repairs, seals and modifies ductwork in Chicago homes to fix uneven temperatures, noisy vents and airflow problems. Ducts carry every bit of heat and cooling from the furnace and AC, and leaks or undersized runs waste much of it. We test static pressure and airflow before recommending anything, then seal, balance and add returns where older homes need them.
Signs a customer needs this: Some rooms are always too hot or too cold; Whistling, rattling or booming from the ducts; A dusty house, or a basement much warmer than the rest of the home in winter; Weak airflow from some registers; AC coils that freeze or furnace limit switches that trip
What's included: Static pressure and room-by-room airflow testing; Sealing accessible leaks with mastic or approved foil tape; Repairing disconnected or crushed runs; Adding or enlarging returns; Balancing dampers and duct insulation
Q: Do you do duct cleaning?
A: We focus on repairs, sealing and airflow. Cleaning helps after construction or contamination, but it doesn’t fix leaky or undersized ducts.
Q: Why is my upstairs always hot in summer?
A: In many Chicago homes, central air was added to ductwork designed only for heat, with few return paths upstairs. Adding returns, sealing and balancing often helps.
Q: Can zoning fix uneven rooms?
A: Sometimes. Zoning with motorized dampers works when the ducts can support it. We test airflow and static pressure first.
Q: Is cloth duct tape OK for sealing ducts?
A: No. Joints and seams should be sealed with mastic or approved foil tape, which hold up far better than cloth duct tape.

## Carbon Monoxide Safety
If a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, leave the home now and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Once responders have cleared the home, Chicago Aircare Pro can do a carbon monoxide safety check to find the source. Every gas furnace, boiler and water heater can produce carbon monoxide (CO) if venting or combustion goes wrong, so we test combustion and venting and check that your CO alarms are where they should be.
Signs a customer needs this: CO alarm sounding or chirping; Headaches or nausea that ease when you leave home; Soot or rust around the furnace, boiler or vent; Yellow or flickering burner flames; A water heater left on the old chimney after a high-efficiency furnace install
What's included: CO readings in the living space and near appliances; Combustion analysis of furnace or boiler flue gases; Venting, chimney draft and liner inspection where visible; Heat exchanger inspection where accessible; Backdraft test with exhaust fans running; CO alarm placement advice
Q: What should I do if my CO alarm goes off?
A: Leave the home immediately and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Once responders say it’s safe, call us to find the source.
Q: My CO alarm chirps but isn’t alarming. What does that mean?
A: A periodic chirp usually means a low battery or an end-of-life alarm. Check its label. A continuous alarm pattern means leave and call 911.
Q: How often should gas appliances be checked?
A: Once a year, before heating season. It’s part of our furnace tune-up and boiler service.
Q: Where should CO alarms go?
A: Illinois law requires them within 15 feet of every sleeping room in most homes with fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage. Follow the alarm maker’s instructions too.
Q: Can a high-efficiency furnace cause CO problems?
A: Any gas appliance can if combustion or venting goes wrong. A common related issue is a water heater left alone on an oversized chimney after a high-efficiency furnace install, which may not draft properly.
