<h2>Check the filter and thermostat</h2><p>A dirty filter or a thermostat set incorrectly is the easiest fix. Make sure it&#x27;s on Cool and set below room temperature.</p><h2>Is the outdoor unit running?</h2><p>If the indoor fan runs but the outdoor unit is silent, the cause is often a failed capacitor or contactor, or a tripped breaker. Capacitors fail often during Chicago heat waves.</p><h2>Look for ice</h2><p>Ice on the copper lines or indoor coil means low airflow or low refrigerant. Turn the AC off and let it thaw, then call for service.</p><h2>Low refrigerant means a leak</h2><p>Air conditioners don&#x27;t use up refrigerant. If yours is low, it has a leak, and adding refrigerant without fixing it is only temporary.</p><h2>Dirty outdoor coil</h2><p>Cottonwood fluff, grass and dirt block the condenser coil, so the system can&#x27;t release heat. A spring tune-up includes cleaning it.</p><h2>Check the breaker and disconnect</h2><p>The outdoor unit has its own breaker and a disconnect box near the condenser. Make sure neither is off. If the breaker trips again after resetting, don&#x27;t keep resetting it, call for service.</p><h2>Clogged condensate line</h2><p>Many systems have a float switch that shuts the AC off if the drain backs up. If there&#x27;s water near your furnace or the AC won&#x27;t start, the drain may be clogged.</p><h2>When it&#x27;s time for a new AC</h2><p>If your system is old, uses R-22 refrigerant or needs a compressor, replacement may be the better investment. We&#x27;ll compare options honestly.</p><h2>Undersized or overworked systems</h2><p>On the hottest days, even a healthy AC may struggle to keep a home more than about 20 degrees below the outdoor temperature. If your system keeps up most of the summer but falls behind during heat waves, it may be normal or slightly undersized. If it can&#x27;t keep up on moderate days, something is wrong.</p><h2>Ductwork and airflow problems</h2><p>Leaky ducts in attics, crushed flex duct or too few returns can keep cool air from reaching rooms. Upstairs rooms are often the first to suffer. A technician can measure airflow and static pressure to find the cause.</p><h2>Preventing summer breakdowns</h2><p>A spring tune-up cleans the outdoor coil, tests capacitors and checks refrigerant, catching the most common causes of summer AC failures before the first heat wave.</p><h2>Thermostat and wiring problems</h2><p>Sometimes the AC itself is fine but the thermostat isn&#x27;t sending the right signal. A failed thermostat, a loose wire or a damaged low-voltage cable between the furnace and the outdoor unit can keep the condenser from starting. Lawn equipment and pets sometimes damage the thin thermostat wire running to the outdoor unit. A technician can test the signal at each point and find the break.</p><h2>Heat waves and system capacity</h2><p>During extended Chicago heat waves, even well-sized air conditioners run constantly and may struggle to hold the set temperature in the late afternoon, especially on upper floors and in rooms with west-facing windows. Closing blinds, avoiding heat-producing appliances during the hottest hours and keeping the filter clean all help. If your home can&#x27;t stay comfortable on typical summer days, that&#x27;s a sign of a system or ductwork problem rather than just hot weather.</p><h2>When to call for service</h2><p>Call for service if the AC is blowing warm air, the outdoor unit isn&#x27;t running, you see ice, water is leaking near the furnace, the breaker trips or you hear grinding, screeching or loud buzzing. These problems rarely fix themselves, and running a struggling system can turn a small repair into a big one. Chicago Aircare Pro offers 24/7 emergency AC repair across Chicago and the north and northwest suburbs.</p><h2>Upstairs rooms that never cool</h2><p>If your main floor is comfortable but upstairs bedrooms stay warm no matter what, the AC may be working fine while the ductwork can&#x27;t deliver enough air upstairs. Hot air rises, attics radiate heat through ceilings and many older Chicago homes have small or few upstairs ducts. Balancing dampers, adding returns, sealing attic ducts or adding a ductless unit upstairs are the usual fixes. A bigger air conditioner rarely solves this problem and can make humidity worse.</p><h2>Short cycling air conditioners</h2><p>An air conditioner that turns on and off every few minutes may be oversized, low on refrigerant, struggling with a dirty coil or reacting to a thermostat in a bad location. Short cycling reduces humidity removal and wears out the compressor, so it&#x27;s worth diagnosing promptly.</p><h2>Getting help fast</h2><p>When your AC stops cooling in the middle of a Chicago summer, you want answers quickly. Our technicians carry common parts like capacitors, contactors and fan motors, so most cooling problems are fixed on the first visit. We&#x27;ll explain what failed, show you if possible and give you options before starting any work, whether that&#x27;s a simple repair or a conversation about replacing an older system.</p>
