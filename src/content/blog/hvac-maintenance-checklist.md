<h2>Every month</h2><p>Check the filter during heating and cooling season, and make sure supply and return vents aren&#x27;t blocked by furniture.</p><h2>Fall</h2><p>Schedule a furnace or boiler tune-up, test CO alarms, replace the humidifier pad and clear the high-efficiency furnace drain.</p><h2>Spring</h2><p>Schedule an AC tune-up, clean around the outdoor unit and check the condensate drain.</p><h2>Leave it to the pros</h2><p>Gas, electrical and refrigerant work should be done by licensed, certified technicians. Our maintenance plan covers a heating and a cooling tune-up every year.</p><h2>Winter</h2><p>Keep vents and the outdoor heat pump unit clear of snow, and watch for ice on high-efficiency furnace vent pipes.</p><h2>Summer</h2><p>Keep plants trimmed back from the outdoor unit and check that the condensate drain is flowing.</p><h2>Keep records</h2><p>Note dates of filter changes and service visits. Records help with warranty claims and spotting problems.</p><h2>Why professional maintenance matters</h2><p>Many HVAC problems can&#x27;t be seen without testing: weak capacitors, gas pressure issues, refrigerant leaks and cracked heat exchangers. A professional tune-up finds them early.</p><h2>Maintenance and warranties</h2><p>Many manufacturers recommend or require regular maintenance to keep warranties valid. Keep records of every visit.</p><h2>The value of a maintenance plan</h2><p>A maintenance plan schedules your visits automatically, gives you priority service and member savings, and keeps your system running at its best.</p><h2>Fall checklist in detail</h2><p>Before the heating season, schedule a furnace or boiler tune-up, replace the filter, test carbon monoxide and smoke alarms, replace the humidifier panel and turn its damper to the winter setting, check that outdoor vent pipes are clear and make sure the area around the furnace is free of stored items.</p><h2>Spring checklist in detail</h2><p>Before cooling season, schedule an AC tune-up, replace the filter, remove any winter cover from the outdoor unit, clear debris and plants, check the condensate drain, turn the humidifier damper to summer and test the AC on a mild day.</p><h2>Why Chicago homes need both seasons</h2><p>Chicago&#x27;s climate puts heavy demand on both heating and cooling. Furnaces and boilers run for months in winter, and air conditioners work hard through humid summers. Skipping either season&#x27;s maintenance increases the risk of a breakdown when you need the system most.</p><h2>What to keep an eye on year-round</h2><p>Listen for new noises, watch for water around equipment, notice rising energy bills and pay attention to rooms that suddenly feel different. Early warning signs often appear weeks before a breakdown.</p><h2>Humidifiers and air cleaners</h2><p>Accessories need maintenance too. Replace humidifier panels each heating season, change media filters on schedule and replace UV lamps yearly.</p><h2>Creating a maintenance routine</h2><p>Set calendar reminders for filter changes and schedule fall and spring tune-ups at the same time each year. A maintenance plan handles scheduling for you, so nothing gets missed.</p><h2>Boiler homes have their own checklist</h2><p>If you heat with a boiler, add these to your fall list: check system pressure when the boiler is cold, bleed radiators that are cool at the top, look for leaks around valves and radiators and make sure the expansion tank isn&#x27;t waterlogged. Steam homeowners should check the water level in the sight glass and make sure radiator valves are fully open. A professional boiler tune-up covers controls, combustion and safety devices.</p><h2>Heat pump homes</h2><p>Heat pumps run year-round, so they need maintenance in both seasons. Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow and leaves, make sure it drains properly during defrost and change filters regularly. A technician should check refrigerant charge, defrost operation and backup heat at least once a year.</p><h2>Ductless systems</h2><p>Clean mini-split filters every few weeks during heavy use and schedule professional deep cleaning of indoor units every year or two to remove buildup on blower wheels and coils.</p><h2>Signs you shouldn&#x27;t wait for the next tune-up</h2><p>Call for service between visits if you notice water around equipment, a burning smell, repeated short cycling, ice on refrigerant lines, banging or screeching noises, a yellow burner flame, a carbon monoxide alarm or a sudden jump in energy bills. These are signs that something needs attention now. Waiting until the next scheduled tune-up can turn a small repair into a larger one, or leave you without heat or cooling at the worst time.</p><h2>Keeping records</h2><p>Keep a simple log of service dates, repairs and filter changes. It helps with warranty claims and gives your technician useful history.</p><h2>Maintenance pays for itself</h2><p>Regular maintenance reduces breakdowns, keeps efficiency high and extends equipment life. When you consider the cost of an emergency repair during a cold snap or heat wave, and the energy wasted by a neglected system, a couple of tune-ups each year are one of the best investments a homeowner can make. Our maintenance plan makes it easy, with scheduled visits, priority service and member savings.</p>
