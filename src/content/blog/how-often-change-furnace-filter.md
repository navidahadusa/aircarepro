<h2>The short answer</h2><p>Standard 1-inch filters usually need changing every 1 to 3 months. Thicker 4 to 5 inch media filters often last 6 to 12 months.</p><h2>Change it more often if…</h2><p>You have pets, allergies, recent renovation dust or a busy household. During peak heating and cooling months, check it monthly.</p><h2>Don&#x27;t overdo the rating</h2><p>Very restrictive filters can choke airflow on some systems. A mid-range MERV rating is a good balance for most homes, or a properly sized media cabinet if you want better filtration.</p><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>A clogged filter makes your system work harder, raises energy bills and can cause furnaces to overheat or AC coils to freeze.</p><h2>How to change your filter</h2><p>Turn the system off, slide out the old filter and note the arrow showing airflow direction. Insert the new filter with the arrow pointing toward the furnace. Write the date on the edge so you remember when it was changed.</p><h2>Where is my filter?</h2><p>Most filters are in a slot between the return duct and the furnace. Some homes have filters behind a return grille in a wall or ceiling. If you can&#x27;t find it, we&#x27;ll show you during your next visit.</p><h2>Signs your filter needs changing</h2><p>Visible dust buildup, reduced airflow, more dust in the house, or a system that runs longer than usual are all signs. A clean filter is the single cheapest thing you can do for your HVAC system.</p><h2>Understanding MERV ratings</h2><p>MERV ratings run from 1 to 16 for residential filters. Basic fiberglass filters are around MERV 1 to 4 and mainly protect equipment. Pleated filters in the MERV 8 to 11 range capture more dust, pollen and pet dander. MERV 13 filters capture finer particles but can restrict airflow on systems not designed for them, especially in thin 1-inch sizes.</p><h2>Media cabinets for better filtration</h2><p>If you want high filtration without restricting airflow, a 4 or 5 inch media cabinet is the answer. Its larger surface area lets air pass easily while capturing fine particles, and filters last much longer.</p><h2>What happens if you don&#x27;t change it</h2><p>A clogged filter reduces airflow, which can cause furnaces to overheat and shut down, AC coils to freeze, blower motors to work harder and energy bills to rise. It can also let dust bypass the filter and coat the coil.</p><h2>Filters during construction and renovation</h2><p>Drywall dust, sawdust and plaster dust are extremely fine and can clog a filter in days rather than months. If you&#x27;re renovating, check your filter weekly and change it as soon as it looks gray. Better yet, run the system as little as possible during the dustiest work and seal off supply and return vents in the work area. Construction dust that gets past the filter can coat the blower wheel and the cooling coil, reducing airflow and efficiency long after the project is finished. After a renovation, a professional inspection of the blower and coil is a smart idea.</p><h2>Filters for allergy and asthma sufferers</h2><p>If someone in your home has allergies or asthma, filtration matters more. A pleated filter in the MERV 8 to 11 range captures much more pollen, dust and pet dander than a basic fiberglass filter, and a media cabinet with a MERV 13 filter captures even finer particles without restricting airflow. Change filters on schedule, keep humidity balanced to reduce dust mites and consider a whole-home air cleaner for the best results. We can recommend the right setup for your system and your family&#x27;s needs.</p><h2>Filters and system warranties</h2><p>Many equipment warranties assume reasonable maintenance, and a severely neglected filter can lead to failures that aren&#x27;t covered. Keeping your filter clean protects not just comfort and efficiency, but also your investment in the equipment. Writing the date on each filter and setting a phone reminder are easy ways to stay on track. Members of our maintenance plan also get filter checks during each tune-up visit.</p><h2>Filters and pets</h2><p>Dogs and cats shed hair and dander year-round, and both collect on furnace filters quickly. In homes with pets, check 1-inch filters every month and expect to change them more often than the package suggests. A pleated filter captures dander better than fiberglass, which helps family members with allergies and keeps hair from building up on the blower wheel and coil.</p><h2>Buying filters in bulk</h2><p>Buying a year&#x27;s supply of the right size saves trips to the store and makes it more likely you&#x27;ll actually change the filter on time. Store them flat in a dry place near the furnace so they&#x27;re ready when you need them.</p><h2>A simple habit with big payoffs</h2><p>Changing your furnace filter takes less than five minutes, yet it protects your equipment, lowers energy bills and improves indoor air. It&#x27;s the single easiest piece of HVAC maintenance any homeowner can do, and it pays off every month of the year.</p>
