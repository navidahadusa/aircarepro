<h2>What changed</h2><p>Under the EPA&#x27;s rules implementing the AIM Act, new residential air conditioners and heat pumps are moving to refrigerants with lower global warming potential. Many manufacturers now use R-454B, and some use R-32, in place of R-410A for new equipment.</p><h2>What it means for your current system</h2><p>If you have an R-410A system, it can still be repaired and serviced, and R-410A remains available for that. You don&#x27;t need to replace a working system just because the refrigerant changed.</p><h2>What about R-22?</h2><p>R-22 has been out of production for years. Older R-22 systems can be serviced with recovered refrigerant, but it&#x27;s expensive. For major repairs, replacement is usually the better choice.</p><h2>A2L refrigerants and safety</h2><p>R-454B and R-32 are classified as A2L, meaning mildly flammable. Equipment is designed with safety features such as leak detection, and installers need proper training and procedures.</p><h2>What it means when you replace</h2><p>New systems use new refrigerants, and indoor coils must match the outdoor unit. Mixing old and new components isn&#x27;t allowed, so replacement often includes a new coil.</p><h2>Our approach</h2><p>We&#x27;ll explain your options clearly, whether repairing your current system makes sense or whether it&#x27;s time for a new, efficient system.</p><h2>Why refrigerants keep changing</h2><p>Refrigerants have changed several times over the decades as scientists and regulators learned more about their environmental effects. Older refrigerants like R-22 harmed the ozone layer and were phased out. R-410A doesn&#x27;t affect ozone but has a high global warming potential, so it is now being phased down for new equipment in favor of refrigerants like R-454B and R-32.</p><h2>What A2L means in practice</h2><p>A2L refrigerants are classified as mildly flammable. They are much harder to ignite than common fuels and are used safely in equipment designed for them. New systems include safety features, and installers follow updated codes and procedures for handling, brazing and leak detection. When properly installed, these systems are safe for your home.</p><h2>Repairing existing R-410A systems</h2><p>If your current air conditioner uses R-410A, it can still be serviced, and R-410A remains available for repairs. You don&#x27;t need to replace a working system because of the refrigerant change. However, over time the cost of R-410A may change as supply shifts, which is one more factor to consider when deciding between a major repair and replacement.</p><h2>What to expect when replacing</h2><p>When you replace your air conditioner or heat pump, the new outdoor unit and indoor coil must be designed for the same refrigerant. That often means replacing the indoor coil and sometimes the line set. We&#x27;ll explain what your installation needs and why.</p><h2>Choosing an installer</h2><p>With new refrigerants and safety requirements, choosing a trained, certified installer is more important than ever. Our technicians are EPA 608 certified and follow manufacturer requirements for every installation.</p><h2>What to ask when replacing</h2><p>Ask which refrigerant the new system uses, whether the indoor coil and line set will be replaced, what safety features the equipment includes and whether the installer is trained on A2L refrigerants. These questions help ensure your new system is installed correctly and performs as designed.</p><h2>Leak detection in new systems</h2><p>Many new A2L systems include refrigerant leak detection sensors that can shut down the system or run the blower to disperse refrigerant if a leak occurs. Proper installation and testing of these features is part of a code-compliant install.</p><h2>How this affects repair decisions</h2><p>If your R-410A air conditioner needs a major repair, such as a compressor or coil, consider the system&#x27;s age, efficiency and repair cost before deciding. Repairing a younger system usually makes sense. For an older system with a major failure, replacing with a new, efficient system using current refrigerant may be the better long-term investment. We&#x27;ll lay out both options with straight recommendations.</p><h2>Heat pumps use the same refrigerants</h2><p>New heat pumps are also moving to lower-GWP refrigerants. The same principles apply: existing systems can be serviced, and new systems require matched components and trained installers.</p><h2>The bottom line</h2><p>The move to new refrigerants doesn&#x27;t mean you need to replace a working air conditioner. Existing systems can still be repaired and maintained. When it is time to replace, new systems using R-454B or similar refrigerants are efficient, safe and designed to meet current standards. Choose an experienced, certified installer, and ask questions about the refrigerant, coil and safety features so you know exactly what you&#x27;re getting.</p>
