The house is getting colder, the thermostat says heat, and nothing is coming out of the vents. It's a lousy feeling, especially at night. Here's what we tell people on the phone while we're on the way: a few checks that sometimes get the heat back, and how to keep the house warm and safe until a technician gets there.

## First, five minutes of checks

These solve more no-heat calls than you'd think:

1. **Thermostat.** Set to *Heat*, not *Cool* or *Off*, and a few degrees above the room temperature. If the screen is blank, replace the batteries.
2. **Furnace switch.** Near the furnace there's a switch that looks like a light switch. Make sure it's on. People flip it by accident.
3. **Breaker.** Check the furnace breaker in the panel. If it tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off and call.
4. **Filter.** A filter caked with dust can make the furnace overheat and shut itself off. Swap it if you have one.
5. **Vent pipes outside.** High-efficiency furnaces vent through two plastic pipes on a side wall. Snow or ice over them shuts the furnace down. Clear them gently by hand. More in our guide on a [furnace vent blocked by snow](/furnace-vent-blocked-by-snow/).

Still nothing? Write down the blinking light code if the furnace door has a little window, and call. Our guide to a [furnace that won't turn on](/furnace-wont-turn-on/) explains what the common causes are, from the igniter to the [inducer motor](/furnace-inducer-motor-replacement/).

## Keep the heat you have

The house is a big battery of warmth. The trick is to stop letting it leak out:

- **Pick one room** to keep warm, ideally an inside room with few windows, and close its door.
- **Close doors** to rooms you're not using.
- **Shut curtains and blinds** after dark. Open the ones on the sunny side during the day.
- **Block drafts** under doors with a rolled towel.
- **Dress in layers** and use blankets. Hats help more than you'd think.

## Space heaters: the safe way

An electric space heater can carry one room through the night if you follow the rules. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's [winter heating safety tips](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2026/Keep-Warm-and-Safe-This-Winter-Tips-for-Using-Generators-Furnaces-and-Space-Heaters) come down to this:

- **Plug it straight into a wall outlet**, not an extension cord or power strip.
- **Keep 3 feet clear** around it: curtains, bedding, furniture, papers.
- **Turn it off** when you leave the room or go to sleep.
- **Set it on a hard, flat floor** where it can't get knocked over.
- **Feel the plug and outlet** now and then. If either is hot, unplug it.

**Never** use a gas stove, oven, grill or a generator indoors for heat. They can fill the house with carbon monoxide, and you won't smell it. Make sure your CO alarms work; here's [where they go and what to do if one sounds](/carbon-monoxide-safety-tips/).

## Protect the pipes

In a long outage on a sub-zero night, pipes on outside walls are the first to freeze:

- **Open the cabinet doors** under kitchen and bathroom sinks on outside walls so room air reaches the pipes.
- **Let a faucet drip** on the coldest lines. Moving water is slower to freeze.
- **Don't shut off the water heater.**
- If a pipe is already frozen, don't thaw it with an open flame.

## When it's an emergency

Call right away, day or night, if:

- it's below freezing and the house keeps dropping toward the 50s;
- there's a baby, an older adult or someone with a health condition at home;
- a **carbon monoxide alarm** is sounding (get everyone outside first and call 911 or the gas utility from outside);
- you smell gas (leave the house and call the gas utility from outside).

In Chicago, the City says to [call 311 if you have no heat](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/oem/provdrs/emerg_mang/news/2026/November/WinterWeatherPreparedness2026.html); they can connect you with a warming center or a ride to one, and they take reports of inadequate heat in rental buildings.
