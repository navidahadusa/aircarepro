<h2>Boiler won&#x27;t fire at all</h2><p>If the boiler is completely off, check the thermostat, the boiler&#x27;s power switch and the breaker. Many boilers have an emergency switch at the top of the basement stairs. Check that it&#x27;s on. If power is fine, the problem may be ignition, a safety control such as a low water cut-off or a failed control board.</p><h2>Boiler runs but radiators stay cold</h2><p>If the boiler is hot but radiators are cold, the circulator pump may have failed, a zone valve may be stuck closed or air may be trapped in the system. Pumps can fail without much warning, and they&#x27;re one of the most common boiler repairs we make.</p><h2>Some radiators heat, others don&#x27;t</h2><p>Uneven heat often comes from air in the radiators, a failed zone valve or balancing problems. Bleeding radiators may help on hot water systems. On steam systems, failed air vents on radiators or mains are a common cause.</p><h2>Pilot light or ignition problems</h2><p>Older boilers with standing pilots may have a weak thermocouple that lets the pilot go out. Newer boilers with electronic ignition can have igniter or flame sensor issues. Either way, a technician can test and replace the faulty part.</p><h2>Control and wiring failures</h2><p>Boilers have relays, aquastats and control boards that can fail. A technician tests each component to find the one that&#x27;s not doing its job.</p><h2>Safety first</h2><p>If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility from outside. If your carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone out and call 911.</p><h2>Circulator pump failures</h2><p>Circulator pumps move hot water from the boiler to your radiators. When a pump&#x27;s motor or bearings fail, the boiler can get hot while the radiators stay cold. Sometimes the pump hums but doesn&#x27;t spin; other times it&#x27;s silent. Pumps are one of the most common boiler repairs we make, and we carry replacement pumps for many systems. Replacing a pump typically restores heat the same day.</p><h2>Zone valves and controls</h2><p>Homes with multiple zones use zone valves or multiple pumps controlled by a zone panel. A failed zone valve motor or end switch can leave one zone cold while others heat, or keep the boiler from firing at all. We test each valve and control to find the problem.</p><h2>Low water cut-offs</h2><p>Many boilers have a low water cut-off that shuts the burner down if water drops too low. If your system has a leak or low pressure, the cut-off may keep the boiler from firing. Steam boilers in particular rely on proper water levels, which should be checked regularly.</p><h2>Older boilers and reliability</h2><p>If your boiler is very old and repairs are becoming frequent, it may be time to consider a replacement. Modern boilers are more efficient and reliable, and we size them to your home&#x27;s actual heat loss.</p><h2>Thermostat and wiring checks</h2><p>Before assuming the boiler has failed, check the thermostat batteries and settings. In zoned systems, each thermostat controls its own zone, so one cold area may simply mean one thermostat isn&#x27;t calling. We test thermostats, wiring and zone controls as part of every no-heat diagnosis.</p><h2>Getting heat back quickly</h2><p>Most no-heat boiler calls are fixed in a single visit. Our technicians carry common parts like circulators, thermocouples, igniters and relays, and we&#x27;ll explain the repair before we start.</p><h2>Pilot lights on older boilers</h2><p>Older boilers with standing pilots rely on a thermocouple or thermopile to keep the gas valve open. When these parts weaken, the pilot goes out and the boiler stops. Relighting the pilot may get you through a night, but if it keeps going out, the thermocouple or pilot assembly needs replacement. Always follow the lighting instructions on the boiler, and if you smell gas, don&#x27;t attempt to relight; leave and call your gas utility.</p><h2>Plan for boiler maintenance</h2><p>A yearly boiler tune-up catches failing pumps, controls and ignition components before they leave you without heat.</p><h2>The bottom line</h2><p>When a boiler stops heating, the cause is usually one component: a pump, a valve, an igniter, a control or a safety device doing its job. A systematic diagnosis finds it quickly. With experience on everything from century-old cast-iron boilers to modern wall-hung condensing units, our technicians can usually restore heat the same day and help you plan for the future if your boiler is nearing the end of its life.</p>
