Thanksgiving is the one day a year many Chicago homes run their heating system the hardest and notice it least. The oven runs for hours, a dozen people fill the living room, doors open and close all afternoon, and guest rooms that sat closed since spring suddenly need to be warm. A little prep this week keeps the day comfortable and keeps you from calling for a repair with a turkey in the oven.

## A few days before: check the system

**Run the heat for a full cycle.** If the furnace or boiler is going to give trouble, you want to know on Monday, not Thursday morning. Set the thermostat a few degrees higher than the room and listen: does it start, run and shut off normally? Do all the rooms warm up?

**Change the furnace filter.** A clogged filter makes the furnace work harder and can make it shut itself off on a long run. If you can't remember the last change, it's probably time. Our guide on [how often to change a furnace filter](/how-often-change-furnace-filter/) explains what to look for.

**Test the carbon monoxide alarms.** With the furnace, the water heater and a gas oven and stove all running for hours, this is the week to press the test button on every CO alarm and replace any past its expiration date. See [carbon monoxide safety](/carbon-monoxide-safety/).

**Clear around the furnace and the vents.** No coats, boxes or folding chairs piled against the furnace or the water heater. Outside, make sure the furnace's PVC vent pipes are clear if it has snowed (see [furnace vent blocked by snow](/furnace-vent-blocked-by-snow/)).

If something sounds off (a burner that clicks without lighting, a furnace that starts and stops every few minutes, a boiler that won't fire), that's worth a service call before the holiday, not after. A [furnace tune-up](/furnace-tune-up/) covers the safety checks and cleaning in one visit.

## The day before: get the guest rooms ready

Rooms that stay closed most of the year are often the coldest in the house:

- Open the door and leave it open overnight so the room joins the rest of the house.
- Open the floor or wall vents, or turn on the radiator valve.
- Move rugs, beds and dressers off the registers and away from radiators.
- In a bungalow with a finished attic, expect the upstairs to run cooler. A space heater left running unattended overnight isn't a safe fix; if it's a regular problem, ask us about a [ductless mini-split](/mini-split-installation/) for that room.

## On the day: thermostat settings for a full house

Start the morning at your normal winter setting. Once the oven has been on for an hour and people start arriving, **lower the thermostat a degree or two**. Cooking and a crowd add a lot of heat, and a house that felt right at 9 a.m. can feel stuffy by 3 p.m.

Watch where the thermostat is. If it's in the kitchen, or in the living room next to where everyone is gathered, it reads warm and the furnace stops early while bedrooms and the basement cool off. A smart thermostat with a remote sensor, or simply checking the other rooms, avoids that.

**Smart and programmable thermostats:** if your schedule normally drops the temperature during the day because nobody's home, override it or set a "hold" for Thursday.

**Two-flats with a shared boiler:** if family is staying in the other unit, remember that both floors often share one thermostat or one boiler. Agree on the setting before the guests get there.

## If you're traveling instead

If you're leaving for the long weekend, **don't turn the heat off**. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated basements and crawl spaces can freeze in a Chicago cold snap, and a burst pipe is a far bigger problem than a gas bill. A setting in the mid-50s or higher is the common recommendation. Leave cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls open, and ask a neighbor or relative to check the house if you'll be gone more than a day or two. Our guide to the [best thermostat settings for a Chicago winter](/best-temperature-chicago-winter/) has more detail.

## If the heat goes out on Thanksgiving

Check the thermostat (Heat mode, batteries), the furnace's power switch and breaker, and the filter. If that doesn't bring it back, [furnace won't turn on](/furnace-wont-turn-on/) walks through the next steps. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call your gas utility from outside. If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone out and call 911.
