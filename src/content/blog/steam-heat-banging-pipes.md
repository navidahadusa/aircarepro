<h2>What causes the banging</h2><p>In a steam system, steam travels through pipes to the radiators, condenses back into water and flows back to the boiler. When water gets trapped in a pipe and steam rushes over it, it can slam the water into fittings, causing loud banging known as water hammer.</p><h2>Sagging or mis-pitched pipes</h2><p>Steam pipes need a slight pitch so condensate drains back to the boiler. Over decades, pipes can sag or hangers can loosen, creating low spots where water collects. Re-pitching pipes is often the fix.</p><h2>Radiators that aren&#x27;t tilted properly</h2><p>One-pipe steam radiators should tilt slightly toward the supply valve so water can drain. A radiator that&#x27;s level or tilted the wrong way can trap water and bang.</p><h2>Partially closed radiator valves</h2><p>On one-pipe systems, radiator valves should be fully open or fully closed. A partially open valve traps condensate and causes noise.</p><h2>Pressure set too high</h2><p>Residential steam systems operate at very low pressure. When the pressure control is set too high, steam moves too fast and noise increases. Adjusting the pressure control helps.</p><h2>Wet steam from the boiler</h2><p>Dirty boiler water, an overfilled boiler or poor near-boiler piping can produce wet steam that carries water into the pipes. Skimming the boiler and correcting piping can solve it.</p><h2>Steam heat in Chicago buildings</h2><p>Steam heat is common in older Chicago buildings, from single-family homes to courtyard apartment buildings. When it works well, steam provides comfortable, even heat. When something is out of balance, it can be noisy, uneven and inefficient. Most steam problems come from a handful of causes: pipe pitch, air vents, pressure settings, water level and near-boiler piping.</p><h2>Air vents on radiators and mains</h2><p>One-pipe steam radiators have air vents that let air escape so steam can enter. Main vents in the basement do the same for the piping. Clogged or failed vents keep steam out, causing cold radiators and uneven heat. Replacing vents with properly sized ones can balance heat throughout the building.</p><h2>Uneven heat between floors</h2><p>If the top floor is too hot while the first floor is cold, or vice versa, venting and pressure adjustments can help balance the system. We evaluate the whole building rather than just one radiator.</p><h2>Skimming and water quality</h2><p>Oil and debris in a steam boiler cause surging water levels and wet steam, which leads to banging and uneven heat. Skimming the boiler removes contaminants and stabilizes operation, especially after a new boiler installation or major repair.</p><h2>When to replace a steam boiler</h2><p>If your steam boiler is leaking, very old or repeatedly failing, replacement may be the best option. A new steam boiler must be sized to the radiation in your building and piped correctly to deliver dry steam.</p><h2>Checking your steam boiler water level</h2><p>Steam boilers have a sight glass that shows the water level. It should be around the middle while the boiler is off. Too low can trigger the low water cut-off; too high can cause wet steam and banging. If the level moves wildly while the boiler runs, the boiler may need skimming.</p><h2>Insulating steam pipes</h2><p>Insulating steam mains in the basement reduces condensation in the pipes, which can reduce banging and help steam reach radiators faster.</p><h2>Getting a quieter steam system</h2><p>Most steam noise can be reduced significantly with the right fixes: re-pitching sagging pipes, tilting radiators correctly, replacing failed air vents, lowering pressure settings and skimming the boiler. These are often relatively simple repairs that make a big difference in comfort and sleep. In older Chicago buildings, a technician who understands steam can transform a noisy, uneven system into a quiet, comfortable one without replacing the boiler.</p><h2>Can steam systems be efficient?</h2><p>Steam systems are often less efficient than modern hot water systems, but a well-tuned steam system with properly sized vents, correct pressure, insulated mains and a right-sized boiler can perform surprisingly well. Many Chicago building owners improve comfort and reduce gas use simply by fixing venting and pressure problems.</p><h2>Converting from steam to hot water</h2><p>Converting a steam system to hot water is possible but usually involves significant piping changes. For most buildings, keeping steam and making it work properly is the more practical choice. We&#x27;ll give you straight advice.</p>
