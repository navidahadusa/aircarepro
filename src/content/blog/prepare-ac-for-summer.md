<h2>Clear around the outdoor unit</h2><p>Remove leaves, debris and plants so there are a couple of feet of open space around the condenser.</p><h2>Replace the filter</h2><p>Start the season with a fresh filter.</p><h2>Check the drain</h2><p>Make sure the condensate line near your furnace is clear so it doesn&#x27;t overflow.</p><h2>Schedule a tune-up</h2><p>A professional tune-up checks refrigerant, electrical parts and coils so you don&#x27;t find a problem on the first 90-degree day.</p><h2>Test it before it&#x27;s hot</h2><p>Turn on your AC on a mild spring day to make sure it starts and cools. Finding a problem before the first heat wave means faster service.</p><h2>Check the thermostat</h2><p>Replace thermostat batteries and set your summer schedule.</p><h2>Look at the insulation on lines</h2><p>The larger copper line should be insulated. Replace damaged insulation to improve efficiency.</p><h2>Remove winter covers</h2><p>If you covered your outdoor unit for winter, remove the cover before turning the AC on.</p><h2>Check the outdoor unit for damage</h2><p>Look for bent fins, damaged lines or debris inside the unit. Call a professional if you see damage.</p><h2>Plan for heat waves</h2><p>Book your tune-up early, before demand spikes during the first hot week.</p><h2>What happens during a professional tune-up</h2><p>A technician cleans the outdoor coil, tests capacitors and contactors, measures motor amperage, checks refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling, clears the condensate drain, checks the filter and measures the temperature drop across the indoor coil. Weak parts can be replaced before they fail on a hot day, and you get a summary of your system&#x27;s condition.</p><h2>Signs your AC needs attention now</h2><p>Warm air from vents, ice on refrigerant lines, water around the furnace, buzzing or rattling from the outdoor unit, short cycling and rising electric bills are all signs to call for service rather than waiting for a full breakdown.</p><h2>Check your outdoor unit&#x27;s surroundings</h2><p>Over winter, leaves, branches and debris can collect around and inside the outdoor unit. Shrubs grow, and items get stored next to the condenser. Clear at least two feet around the unit and make sure nothing is blocking the top where air exits. Good airflow helps the unit release heat efficiently.</p><h2>Think about your thermostat schedule</h2><p>Spring is a good time to set up a cooling schedule. Raising the temperature a few degrees when you&#x27;re away and using ceiling fans for comfort can reduce electricity use. Smart thermostats make this easy.</p><h2>Inspect visible ductwork</h2><p>If you have an unfinished basement or accessible ducts, look for disconnected sections or gaps. Leaky ducts in unconditioned spaces waste cooling.</p><h2>Plan for upgrades</h2><p>If your AC is aging, spring is the best time to plan a replacement. Installers have more availability before summer demand peaks, and you won&#x27;t be rushed into a decision during a heat wave.</p><h2>Check the electrical disconnect</h2><p>Near your outdoor unit there&#x27;s a disconnect box with a pull-out block or switch. Make sure it&#x27;s in the on position and the cover is intact. If you see burned or melted parts, don&#x27;t touch anything and call for service.</p><h2>Clean supply and return vents</h2><p>Vacuum dust from supply registers and return grilles, and make sure furniture, rugs and curtains aren&#x27;t blocking them. Blocked returns are a common cause of poor airflow and higher energy bills in summer.</p><h2>Know your system&#x27;s age</h2><p>Look for the data plate on your outdoor unit to find the model and serial number. Knowing your system&#x27;s age helps you plan ahead. Air conditioners around 12 to 15 years old or older, especially those using R-22, are worth discussing with your technician during the spring visit.</p><h2>Humidity control for Chicago summers</h2><p>Chicago summers aren&#x27;t just hot; they&#x27;re humid. An air conditioner that&#x27;s properly sized and charged removes moisture as it cools, making your home feel comfortable at a higher thermostat setting. If your home feels clammy even when the AC runs, the system may be oversized, low on refrigerant or running with the fan set to On instead of Auto. Setting the fan to Auto lets moisture drain from the coil instead of being blown back into the house. A whole-home dehumidifier can help in especially humid homes and basements.</p><h2>Cottonwood season</h2><p>In late spring, cottonwood fluff can coat outdoor coils in a matter of days. Check your outdoor unit after cottonwood season and gently rinse the coil from the inside out with a garden hose, with the power off, or schedule a professional cleaning.</p><h2>Schedule early, stay cool</h2><p>Spring tune-up appointments fill up fast once the first warm days arrive. Booking in March or April means more flexible scheduling and peace of mind that your system is ready. Members of our maintenance plan are scheduled automatically and get priority service if anything goes wrong during the summer.</p>
