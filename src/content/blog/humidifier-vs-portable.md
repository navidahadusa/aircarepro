<h2>Portable humidifiers</h2><p>Portable units are inexpensive and easy to start using, but they treat one room at a time, need frequent refilling and must be cleaned regularly to prevent mold and bacteria.</p><h2>Whole-home humidifiers</h2><p>Installed on your furnace, a whole-home humidifier adds moisture to every room automatically, connected to your water supply so there&#x27;s nothing to fill.</p><h2>Maintenance</h2><p>Whole-home bypass and fan-powered units need a new water panel each season. Steam units need periodic cleaning. Portables need daily attention.</p><h2>Control</h2><p>Whole-home systems use humidistats, often with outdoor sensors, to keep humidity balanced and avoid window condensation.</p><h2>Limitations</h2><p>Whole-home humidifiers require forced-air heating. Homes with boilers may rely on portable or room units.</p><h2>Which is right for you?</h2><p>For forced-air homes, whole-home humidifiers are more convenient and effective. We&#x27;ll recommend the right type and size.</p><h2>Health considerations</h2><p>Portable humidifiers can grow mold and bacteria if not cleaned frequently, and some types release minerals from tap water into the air as white dust. Whole-home humidifiers connected to your furnace avoid standing water in living spaces, and their panels are replaced each season to keep them clean.</p><h2>Coverage and consistency</h2><p>A portable unit treats one room, often leaving the rest of the home dry. Humidity also drops as soon as the tank runs empty. A whole-home system maintains steady humidity throughout the house, all season long.</p><h2>Noise</h2><p>Portable humidifiers can be noisy, especially at night. Whole-home units operate quietly in the basement or utility room.</p><h2>Energy and water use</h2><p>Whole-home humidifiers use a small amount of electricity for the solenoid and controls, plus water. Steam units use more electricity but deliver precise control. Portables use electricity as well, and several units may be needed to cover a whole house.</p><h2>Installation and maintenance</h2><p>Whole-home humidifiers require professional installation, connecting to your ductwork, water supply, drain and furnace controls. After that, maintenance is usually just a yearly water panel replacement and a quick check during your tune-up.</p><h2>Our recommendation</h2><p>For forced-air homes in Chicago, a whole-home humidifier is usually the better long-term choice for comfort, health and convenience.</p><h2>Bypass, fan-powered or steam?</h2><p>Bypass humidifiers are the most common and affordable. Fan-powered models deliver more moisture without a bypass duct. Steam humidifiers provide the highest output and the most precise control and work even when the furnace isn&#x27;t heating, making them ideal for larger homes and heat pump systems.</p><h2>Signs your humidifier isn&#x27;t working</h2><p>If static shocks and dry skin return, or a hygrometer shows humidity well below your setting, the water panel may be clogged, the solenoid valve may have failed or the damper may be in the summer position. We can diagnose and repair existing humidifiers.</p><h2>Seasonal settings</h2><p>Set bypass humidifier dampers to the winter position in fall and summer position in spring. Turn off the water supply or set the humidistat to off in summer.</p><h2>Real example: a Chicago bungalow</h2><p>In a typical Chicago bungalow with forced-air heat, homeowners often run two or three portable humidifiers in winter and still wake up with dry throats. After installing a bypass humidifier with an automatic control on the furnace, humidity stays steady throughout the house without daily refilling. The upstairs bedrooms, which portable units never reached, become comfortable too.</p><h2>Humidifiers and heat pumps</h2><p>Heat pumps deliver air at lower temperatures than furnaces, which reduces the output of bypass and fan-powered humidifiers. Steam humidifiers work well with heat pumps because they generate their own moisture independently of supply air temperature.</p><h2>Maintenance tips</h2><p>Replace the water panel each fall, check the drain line, make sure the water supply valve is open and set the damper correctly. A quick check during your furnace tune-up keeps everything working.</p><h2>Getting the right size</h2><p>Whole-home humidifiers are sized by how many gallons of water they can add per day, based on your home&#x27;s size and how tightly it&#x27;s built. A leaky older home needs more capacity than a newer, well-sealed one. We size the humidifier to your home so it can keep up on the coldest, driest days without over-humidifying on milder ones.</p><h2>The bottom line</h2><p>For most Chicago homes with forced-air heat, a whole-home humidifier provides better comfort, healthier air and far less hassle than portable units. If you have a boiler, we can suggest other options to keep humidity balanced.</p>
