<h2>What UV lights do</h2><p>UV-C light inhibits the growth of mold and bacteria on surfaces it shines on. In HVAC systems, lamps are aimed at the cooling coil and drain pan, or positioned to treat moving air.</p><h2>Benefits</h2><p>A cleaner coil can help maintain airflow and efficiency and reduce musty odors from biological growth.</p><h2>Limits</h2><p>UV lights don&#x27;t capture dust or pollen. They work alongside good filtration and humidity control, not in place of them.</p><h2>Maintenance</h2><p>UV lamps lose output over time and are typically replaced about once a year.</p><h2>Who benefits most</h2><p>Homes with musty odors when the AC starts, damp mechanical rooms or coils that get dirty quickly benefit most.</p><h2>Our honest advice</h2><p>Not every home needs a UV light. We&#x27;ll inspect your system and tell you whether one makes sense.</p><h2>How UV lights are installed</h2><p>Most coil-sterilizing UV lights mount on the supply plenum or air handler, with the lamp inserted through a small opening so it shines directly on the evaporator coil. A ballast or power supply mounts outside the ductwork and connects to power. Installation is usually quick and doesn&#x27;t require major changes to your system.</p><h2>Safety features</h2><p>UV-C light can harm eyes and skin with direct exposure, which is why lamps are installed inside the system. Many models include indicator lights and safety interlocks. Always turn off power before opening an access panel near a UV lamp.</p><h2>Pairing UV with other air quality upgrades</h2><p>UV lights work best as part of a complete approach to indoor air quality: good filtration to capture particles, balanced humidity to discourage mold and dust mites, and clean, well-maintained equipment. We often recommend UV alongside a media air cleaner or dehumidifier in humid homes.</p><h2>Costs to consider</h2><p>Beyond installation, plan for yearly lamp replacement. Lamps lose effectiveness over time even if they still glow, so following the manufacturer&#x27;s replacement schedule matters.</p><h2>When we don&#x27;t recommend UV</h2><p>If your coil is clean, your system is in a dry location and there are no odor issues, a UV light may add little value. We&#x27;ll tell you honestly.</p><h2>Making the decision</h2><p>If you&#x27;ve noticed musty smells when your AC runs, see growth on your coil or drain pan, or have family members with allergies, ask us to evaluate whether UV makes sense for your home.</p><h2>Real-world example</h2><p>In a basement mechanical room that stays damp all summer, we often find dark growth on the evaporator coil and slime in the drain pan, along with a musty smell every time the AC starts. After cleaning the coil and installing a coil-sterilizing UV light, homeowners frequently report that the smell is gone and the coil stays noticeably cleaner at the next tune-up.</p><h2>UV and humidity control together</h2><p>In very humid homes, pairing a UV light with a dehumidifier attacks the root cause of biological growth, which is moisture, while the UV keeps the coil surface clean.</p><h2>Ask during your tune-up</h2><p>If you&#x27;re curious whether UV makes sense, ask during your next AC tune-up. We&#x27;ll look at your coil and drain pan and give you an honest recommendation.</p><h2>What UV won&#x27;t do</h2><p>UV lights won&#x27;t remove dust, pollen, pet dander or smoke particles from the air, won&#x27;t control humidity and won&#x27;t fix ductwork problems. They also won&#x27;t clean a coil that&#x27;s already heavily dirty; the coil should be cleaned first. Understanding these limits helps you decide whether a UV light is the right investment or whether filtration, humidity control or duct improvements would do more for your home.</p><h2>Choosing a quality system</h2><p>Look for UV systems from established manufacturers with proper certifications, replaceable lamps and status indicators. We install quality systems and position lamps for maximum effectiveness.</p><h2>Questions to ask your technician</h2><p>Ask whether your coil shows signs of biological growth, whether your mechanical room is especially damp, which type of UV light would be most effective for your system, how often lamps need replacing and how UV compares to other air quality options for your specific concerns. A good technician will give you straightforward answers rather than a sales pitch.</p><h2>The bottom line</h2><p>UV lights can be a worthwhile addition in the right situation, especially in humid systems with musty odors or recurring coil growth. In other homes, filtration or humidity control may matter more. We&#x27;ll help you decide.</p>
