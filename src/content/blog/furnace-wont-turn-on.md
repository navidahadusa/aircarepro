<h2>Start with the thermostat</h2><p>More furnace no-start calls than you&#x27;d think come down to the thermostat. Make sure it&#x27;s set to Heat, the set temperature is a few degrees above the room temperature and the screen isn&#x27;t blank. If it runs on batteries, replace them. Smart thermostats can lose power if there&#x27;s no common wire, which can make the furnace stop responding. If you recently changed the thermostat, double-check the wiring against the old one.</p><h2>Check power to the furnace</h2><p>Every furnace has a service switch that looks like a light switch, usually mounted on the furnace or on a nearby wall or ceiling joist. It&#x27;s easy to flip off by accident. Check that it&#x27;s on, then check the circuit breaker labeled furnace in your electrical panel. If the breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, don&#x27;t keep resetting it; call for service.</p><h2>Make sure the door panel is closed</h2><p>Furnaces have a safety switch behind the blower compartment door. If the panel isn&#x27;t seated properly after a filter change, the furnace won&#x27;t run at all. Remove and reinstall the panel firmly and listen for the furnace to start.</p><h2>A clogged filter can shut it down</h2><p>A filter packed with dust restricts airflow so badly that the furnace overheats and trips its high-limit switch. Some furnaces lock out after several limit trips. Replace the filter, wait a few minutes and try again.</p><h2>Igniter and flame sensor failures</h2><p>If you hear the inducer motor start and then the furnace stops without lighting, or it lights for a few seconds and shuts off, the igniter or flame sensor is the likely cause. Hot surface igniters crack with age, and flame sensors get coated with residue. A technician can test and clean or replace them quickly.</p><h2>Pressure switch and venting problems</h2><p>Before the burners light, a pressure switch confirms the vent is clear. A blocked PVC vent pipe outside, a cracked pressure switch hose, a weak inducer motor or a clogged condensate trap can keep the switch from closing. In Chicago winters, snow and ice blocking vent pipes are a common culprit. Clear exterior vents carefully, but leave internal diagnosis to a professional.</p><h2>Control board, gas supply and other causes</h2><p>Less often, the problem is a failed control board, a closed gas valve, a gas supply issue or a blown low-voltage fuse on the board. Many furnaces display a blinking code that points toward the cause. If you can see the code through the sight glass, note the pattern before you call; it helps your technician diagnose faster.</p><h2>Furnaces that stop during cold snaps</h2><p>Many no-start calls in Chicago happen during the first deep cold snap of the season. Parts that were marginal all fall finally give out when the furnace has to run for hours at a time: a weak igniter cracks, a dirty flame sensor stops proving flame, or a condensate line in a cold corner freezes solid. If your furnace stops on the coldest night of the year, it&#x27;s usually not a coincidence. A fall tune-up that cleans the sensor, tests the igniter and clears the drain is the best way to avoid it.</p><h2>What we do on a no-heat call</h2><p>When we arrive, we start by confirming the thermostat is calling for heat, then follow the furnace&#x27;s sequence of operation step by step: inducer, pressure switch, igniter, gas valve, flame sensor and blower. We read any fault codes, check voltages and measure gas pressure and draft. That systematic approach finds the actual failed part quickly. Our vans carry common parts, so most no-heat calls are fixed in a single visit, and we test the furnace through several full cycles before we leave.</p><h2>Keeping your family safe while you wait</h2><p>If your furnace won&#x27;t start in freezing weather, close curtains, keep interior doors open so heat from any working source can spread, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to protect pipes. Never use a gas oven or outdoor heater indoors to stay warm. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside. Call us any time, day or night, for emergency heating service.</p><h2>Don&#x27;t wait if it&#x27;s freezing</h2><p>If the simple checks don&#x27;t get your furnace running and temperatures are dropping, call right away. Pipes can freeze in unheated homes within hours during a Chicago cold snap. We offer 24/7 emergency service across the city and suburbs.</p>
