Every winter, a cold snap leaves some Chicago apartments without heat. If you rent, you want to know what you're owed. If you own a two-flat or a small building, you want to know what the City expects from you, and how to avoid a no-heat call on the coldest night of the year. This guide covers both, in plain language, and points you to the City's official rules.

## What the Chicago heat ordinance requires

The City of Chicago's heat ordinance sets minimum indoor temperatures for rental housing during heat season, which runs from **September 15 through June 1**. During that time, according to the City:

- **8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m.:** at least **68°F** inside the unit
- **10:30 p.m. to 8:30 a.m.:** at least **66°F** inside the unit

The rule applies when the landlord supplies the heat, which is how most Chicago apartment buildings, courtyard buildings, two-flats and three-flats work. The City's Department of Buildings enforces it and can cite landlords who don't comply. For the exact wording, early-fall rules and enforcement details, read the City's official page: [Chicago Heat Ordinance (City of Chicago)](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bldgs/supp_info/chicago-heat-ordinance.html).

## If you rent: what to do when the heat is out

1. **Check what you can.** Is your thermostat set to Heat and a few degrees above the room temperature? If you have your own furnace, is the switch on and the filter reasonably clean? Radiators should be warm when the boiler is running.
2. **Tell your landlord right away**, in writing if you can (text or email), with the date, time and indoor temperature. Keep a record.
3. **Call 311 if the heat isn't fixed.** The City asks residents to contact the landlord first, then call 311 or use the City's 311 app if nothing happens.
4. **Stay safe while you wait.** Don't heat the apartment with an oven or a grill, and use space heaters only as the manufacturer directs. If you smell gas, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911 first.

## If you own a two-flat or small building

For owners, heat season is a responsibility. Older Chicago buildings often run on steam or hot-water boilers that are decades old, and they tend to fail when they're working hardest. A few habits prevent most no-heat emergencies:

- **Tune up the system every fall.** On a boiler, that means checking the burner, the controls, the low-water cutoff, the gauge glass on steam systems, the air vents, and the pumps and zone valves on hot-water systems. On a furnace, it means the igniter, flame sensor, venting and safety controls, plus a carbon monoxide check.
- **Know the warning signs.** Cold radiators on one floor, banging pipes, a boiler that keeps locking out, pressure creeping up, or water around the boiler all mean it's time to call before a cold night turns them into a full outage.
- **Have a plan for nights and weekends.** Keep a 24/7 HVAC number where you and your tenants can find it, and answer tenant heat complaints quickly. A documented, fast response matters.
- **Keep the system's paperwork.** Service records show when equipment was last checked and help a technician diagnose problems faster.
