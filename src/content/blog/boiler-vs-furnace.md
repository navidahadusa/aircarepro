<h2>How they work</h2><p>A furnace heats air and blows it through ducts. A boiler heats water or makes steam that flows to radiators or radiant floors.</p><h2>Why so many Chicago homes have boilers</h2><p>Many two-flats, greystones and older homes were built with radiator heat. Boilers provide quiet, even, comfortable heat and can last a long time.</p><h2>Adding air conditioning</h2><p>Homes with boilers usually lack ductwork. Ductless mini-splits are a popular way to add efficient cooling, and they can provide extra heat in spring and fall.</p><h2>Which is better?</h2><p>Neither is always better. If your boiler works well, keeping it and adding mini-splits is often smarter than tearing out radiators and installing ducts.</p><h2>Maintenance differences</h2><p>Boilers need checks of water pressure, expansion tanks, circulators and venting. Furnaces need filter changes, burner and flame sensor cleaning and blower inspection. Both benefit from yearly professional service.</p><h2>Efficiency options</h2><p>Both come in high-efficiency condensing versions. Modern condensing boilers with outdoor reset controls can be very efficient, especially with lower water temperatures.</p><h2>Humidity and air quality</h2><p>Boilers don&#x27;t move air, so they don&#x27;t dry it out as much, but you also can&#x27;t add a whole-home humidifier or air cleaner without ductwork. Furnaces allow both.</p><h2>Comfort differences</h2><p>Boiler heat warms objects and surfaces in the room, creating a steady, gentle warmth without drafts or blowing air. Furnaces heat air quickly and can respond faster to thermostat changes, but they can create temperature swings and dry the air.</p><h2>Cost of operation</h2><p>Both can be efficient. Modern condensing boilers and furnaces reach high efficiency ratings. Operating cost depends more on efficiency, sizing and your home&#x27;s insulation than on boiler versus furnace.</p><h2>Replacing like with like</h2><p>In most cases, replacing a boiler with a new boiler or a furnace with a new furnace is simplest and least expensive. Switching between them is possible but involves major work.</p><h2>Signs your boiler needs service</h2><p>Cold radiators, banging pipes, leaks, pressure problems and a pilot that won&#x27;t stay lit are all signs to call a technician.</p><h2>Signs your furnace needs service</h2><p>Cold air from vents, short cycling, strange noises, a yellow flame and rising gas bills mean it&#x27;s time for service.</p><h2>Space and installation considerations</h2><p>Modern wall-hung condensing boilers can be remarkably compact, freeing up basement space compared with older floor-standing boilers. Furnaces need room for the unit, filter access and ductwork connections. When replacing either, we look at the available space, venting options and access for service. In tight Chicago basements and utility rooms, the right equipment choice can make a big difference in both installation and long-term maintenance.</p><h2>Two-flats and multi-unit buildings</h2><p>In two-flats and small apartment buildings, boilers are common, sometimes one per unit and sometimes one shared boiler for the whole building. Furnaces are more common when units were converted or rehabbed. Each approach has pros and cons for comfort, control and cost. Owners considering changes should weigh tenant comfort, utility billing and long-term maintenance.</p><h2>Adding cooling to a boiler home</h2><p>The biggest drawback of boiler heat is that it doesn&#x27;t provide air conditioning. Ductless mini-splits solve that neatly, adding efficient cooling and supplemental heat without ductwork. Some homeowners also use high-velocity small-duct systems. We&#x27;ll help you compare options.</p><h2>Which is right for you?</h2><p>If your boiler is in good shape and you love the comfort of radiators, keep it and add mini-splits for cooling. If you already have ducts, a high-efficiency furnace paired with central air is usually best. We&#x27;ll give you honest options for your home.</p><h2>Noise and air movement</h2><p>Boilers are nearly silent in living spaces because there&#x27;s no blower pushing air through vents. Furnaces create some air noise, though modern variable-speed models are much quieter than older ones. Some people love the quiet, radiant warmth of boilers, while others prefer how quickly a furnace responds to a thermostat change. Both can provide excellent comfort when sized and installed correctly.</p><h2>Cost of replacement</h2><p>Replacement cost depends on the equipment, venting, piping or ductwork changes and controls. Replacing like with like is usually the most economical choice. We&#x27;ll give you clear, written options so you can compare.</p><h2>Efficiency at a glance</h2><p>Look for the AFUE rating on any new furnace or boiler. Higher numbers mean more of your gas becomes heat. Condensing models of both types reach the highest ratings, while standard models vent more heat up the chimney. We&#x27;ll explain the difference in real-world terms for your home.</p><h2>Making the right choice for your home</h2><p>The best heating system is the one that fits your home, your comfort preferences and your budget. If you&#x27;re unsure, we&#x27;ll walk through the options with you, look at your existing system and ductwork or piping and give you honest, written recommendations. Whether you keep your boiler, upgrade your furnace or add ductless cooling, the goal is the same: reliable, efficient comfort all year.</p>
