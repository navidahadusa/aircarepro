<h2>Banging or booming at start-up</h2><p>A loud bang or boom when the furnace lights can mean delayed ignition: gas builds up for a moment before it lights. Dirty burners, a weak igniter or incorrect gas pressure can cause it. Delayed ignition stresses the heat exchanger over time and should be checked by a technician.</p><h2>Popping or ticking in the ducts</h2><p>Metal ductwork expands as it heats and contracts as it cools, causing popping or ticking sounds. This is usually normal, though excessive noise can point to undersized ducts or high static pressure.</p><h2>Squealing or screeching</h2><p>High-pitched squealing usually comes from the blower motor or inducer motor bearings, or from a worn belt on older furnaces. Bearings that are failing will eventually seize, so it&#x27;s best to address this before the motor stops.</p><h2>Rattling</h2><p>Rattling can be as simple as a loose access panel or a loose duct connection. It can also point to a failing inducer motor or, in some cases, a cracked heat exchanger. If the rattle is coming from inside the furnace, have it inspected.</p><h2>Humming and buzzing</h2><p>A soft hum from the blower or transformer is normal. Loud buzzing can indicate a failing capacitor, a motor struggling to start or an electrical problem.</p><h2>Repeated clicking</h2><p>A few clicks as the furnace starts are normal relays and gas valves. Repeated clicking without the burners lighting usually means the furnace is trying and failing to ignite, often due to an igniter, flame sensor or gas supply problem.</p><h2>Whistling</h2><p>Whistling often means restricted airflow: a dirty filter, closed registers or undersized returns. Fixing airflow quiets the system and helps it run more efficiently.</p><h2>Noises that mean shut it off now</h2><p>Some sounds mean you should turn the furnace off and call for service right away: loud booming that repeats at every start, grinding or metal-on-metal scraping from the blower or inducer, or a burning electrical smell along with buzzing. Continuing to run the furnace in these cases can cause more damage or create a safety hazard. Turn the thermostat off, switch off the furnace power and call us.</p><h2>Noises that can wait a day</h2><p>Light squealing from a motor, occasional rattling from a loose panel and ductwork popping as it heats usually aren&#x27;t emergencies, but they should be checked soon. Small problems like worn bearings and loose parts get worse over time and are cheaper to fix early.</p><h2>How we track down noises</h2><p>We start by listening during a full heating cycle to pinpoint when and where the sound occurs: at ignition, when the blower starts, during the cycle or at shut-off. Then we inspect the relevant components, measure gas pressure, check motor bearings and look at duct connections. Noises are often clues that lead straight to the cause.</p><h2>Quieter furnaces</h2><p>If your furnace has always been loud, a new variable-speed furnace can be dramatically quieter. Variable-speed blowers ramp up slowly instead of starting at full speed, and two-stage burners run gently most of the time. Duct improvements can also reduce whooshing and whistling.</p><h2>Inducer motor noise</h2><p>The inducer motor runs at the start of every cycle to clear the vent before ignition. As its bearings wear, it can start to whine, rattle or grind. Replacing a failing inducer before it seizes prevents a no-heat call, since the furnace won&#x27;t light without it.</p><h2>Rumbling after shut-off</h2><p>A rumbling sound after the burners shut off can indicate a gas valve that isn&#x27;t closing fully or a dirty burner. This should be checked right away.</p><h2>Whooshing at the vents</h2><p>Loud whooshing from supply registers usually means air is moving too fast through undersized ducts or registers. It&#x27;s common when a larger furnace or air conditioner was installed without updating ductwork. Adding returns, enlarging registers or adjusting blower speed can quiet the system and improve efficiency. A technician can measure static pressure to confirm whether the ductwork is too restrictive.</p><h2>Trust your ears</h2><p>You know what your furnace normally sounds like. When something changes, a new bang, squeal, rattle or hum, it&#x27;s often the first warning that a part is wearing out. Calling early usually means a smaller, less expensive repair and avoids being left without heat on a cold night. Note when the sound happens and where it seems to come from; those details help our technicians find the cause quickly and get your furnace running quietly again.</p>
