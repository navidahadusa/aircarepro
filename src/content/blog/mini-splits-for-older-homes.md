<h2>No ductwork required</h2><p>Mini-splits only need a small hole for refrigerant lines, so they&#x27;re ideal for homes heated by radiators.</p><h2>Room-by-room comfort</h2><p>Each indoor unit has its own control, which is perfect for hot attic bedrooms and cool basements.</p><h2>Efficient heating too</h2><p>Mini-splits are heat pumps, so they can supplement your boiler in spring and fall.</p><h2>Planning matters</h2><p>Placement of indoor heads and line sets affects both performance and appearance. We plan every system carefully.</p><h2>How many zones do you need?</h2><p>Bedrooms, living areas and finished attics are common zones. We&#x27;ll help you decide where indoor units provide the most benefit.</p><h2>Appearance options</h2><p>Wall-mounted units are most common, but floor consoles, ceiling cassettes and slim ducted units are options depending on your home.</p><h2>Maintenance</h2><p>Clean filters regularly and schedule professional cleaning every year or two to keep units efficient and odor-free.</p><h2>Cost compared to central air</h2><p>For homes without ducts, mini-splits are usually less disruptive and often less expensive than installing new ductwork for central air.</p><h2>Efficiency benefits</h2><p>Variable-speed compressors and zone control mean you only cool or heat the rooms you&#x27;re using.</p><h2>Building rules for condos</h2><p>Condo associations may have rules about outdoor units and wall penetrations. We can help you plan within those rules.</p><h2>How a mini-split installation works</h2><p>Installation usually takes one to two days. We mount the indoor units, drill a small hole for the line set, place the outdoor unit on a pad, stand or wall bracket, run and insulate the refrigerant lines, connect power, pressure test, pull a vacuum and charge the system. Then we show you how to use the remote or app.</p><h2>Pairing mini-splits with your boiler</h2><p>Many Chicago homeowners keep their boiler as the main heat source and use mini-splits for cooling and shoulder-season heating. This approach takes advantage of the efficiency of heat pumps when temperatures are mild while keeping reliable boiler heat for the coldest weather.</p><h2>Bungalows and attic bedrooms</h2><p>Chicago bungalows often have finished attic bedrooms that get very hot in summer. A single-zone mini-split is one of the most effective ways to make these rooms comfortable without major construction.</p><h2>Two-flats and multi-unit buildings</h2><p>In two-flats, each unit can have its own mini-split system, giving residents individual control and separate electric usage. This is a popular upgrade for owner-occupied buildings.</p><h2>Noise and comfort</h2><p>Mini-split indoor units are very quiet, often quieter than window units by a wide margin. Variable-speed operation provides steady temperatures without the on-off cycling of older systems.</p><h2>Preserving historic interiors</h2><p>Because mini-splits only need small refrigerant lines, they preserve plaster, trim and original details in historic homes far better than adding ductwork.</p><h2>Line set routes in masonry buildings</h2><p>In brick and stone Chicago buildings, line sets usually exit through a small core hole in the exterior wall and run down the outside to the condenser, covered by a paintable line set cover. Inside, we route lines through closets or behind furniture when possible. Planning these routes carefully keeps the installation neat and protects the building&#x27;s appearance.</p><h2>Condensate drainage</h2><p>Indoor units produce water when cooling. In most installations the drain line runs outside alongside the refrigerant lines. When gravity drainage isn&#x27;t possible, a small condensate pump is used. Proper drainage prevents leaks and water stains.</p><h2>Electrical needs</h2><p>Mini-splits need a dedicated circuit for the outdoor unit. Older homes may need electrical panel work. We check this during the estimate so there are no surprises.</p><h2>Choosing the right brands and models</h2><p>Quality mini-split systems from established manufacturers offer cold-climate models, quiet operation and reliable controls. We help you choose equipment that fits your rooms, budget and heating needs, and we install it according to manufacturer specifications so warranties stay valid. Proper sizing for each room matters: an oversized indoor unit can short cycle and leave a room clammy, while an undersized one can&#x27;t keep up on hot afternoons.</p><h2>Controls and smart features</h2><p>Mini-splits come with remotes, and many offer Wi-Fi control through a phone app or compatible smart thermostats. Schedules, eco modes and room-by-room control make it easy to save energy.</p><h2>Heating with mini-splits in a Chicago winter</h2><p>Cold-climate mini-splits can provide meaningful heat well below freezing, and in spring and fall they often heat more efficiently than an older boiler. Many homeowners use mini-splits to take the chill off rooms in shoulder seasons and let the boiler handle the deep cold of January and February. This approach can lower gas use while keeping the steady radiant comfort of radiators when it matters most.</p><h2>Getting started</h2><p>A free in-home estimate is the first step. We&#x27;ll walk through your home, discuss which rooms need help and design a system that fits your space, style and budget.</p>
