<h2>What a frozen AC looks like</h2><p>You may see ice on the larger copper line at the outdoor unit or near the furnace, frost on the indoor coil if visible, weak airflow from vents and warm air instead of cool. Water may also pool around the furnace as ice melts.</p><h2>What to do right away</h2><p>Turn the thermostat to Off and set the fan to On. This lets the blower help thaw the coil. Don&#x27;t chip at the ice. Once it&#x27;s thawed, replace the filter and call for service if the problem returns.</p><h2>Restricted airflow</h2><p>The indoor coil needs steady airflow to absorb heat. A clogged filter, closed registers, a dirty coil or a failing blower motor reduce airflow, letting the coil drop below freezing.</p><h2>Low refrigerant</h2><p>Low refrigerant lowers pressure in the coil, which drops its temperature below freezing. Since AC systems don&#x27;t use up refrigerant, a low charge means there&#x27;s a leak that needs repair.</p><h2>Running in cool weather</h2><p>Running the AC when it&#x27;s cool outside, especially at night, can cause freezing on some systems. Avoid running it below the manufacturer&#x27;s recommended outdoor temperature.</p><h2>Professional diagnosis</h2><p>A technician checks airflow, measures refrigerant charge with superheat and subcooling, inspects the coil and tests the blower to find and fix the cause.</p><h2>Why frozen ACs are common in Chicago summers</h2><p>Chicago&#x27;s humid summers make air conditioners work hard to remove moisture. When airflow is restricted, the indoor coil gets colder and that moisture freezes onto the coil instead of draining away. Once a layer of ice forms, it blocks even more airflow, and the problem snowballs until the whole coil is encased in ice. We see this most often in homes with clogged filters, closed vents in unused rooms and older systems with dirty coils.</p><h2>Blower motor and capacitor problems</h2><p>If the blower motor is weak or its capacitor is failing, the fan may run slowly or not at all while the outdoor unit keeps running. With little air moving across it, the coil freezes quickly. Variable-speed blower modules can also fail. We test the blower and its components as part of every frozen-coil call.</p><h2>Dirty evaporator coils</h2><p>Over time, dust that slips past the filter collects on the indoor coil, acting like insulation and blocking airflow. A dirty coil reduces cooling and can lead to freezing. Professional coil cleaning restores performance. UV lights can help keep coils cleaner in humid systems.</p><h2>Refrigerant leaks and the compressor</h2><p>Running an AC low on refrigerant not only causes freezing but also risks overheating the compressor, the most expensive part of your system. If your AC has frozen more than once, a refrigerant leak is a strong possibility. Our EPA 608 certified technicians locate and repair leaks properly.</p><h2>Preventing frozen coils</h2><p>Change filters regularly, keep supply and return vents open, schedule a spring tune-up and don&#x27;t run your AC when it&#x27;s cool outside. These steps prevent most freezing problems.</p><h2>How we fix a frozen AC</h2><p>After the system thaws, we measure airflow and static pressure, check the filter and coil, test the blower and capacitor, and measure refrigerant charge with superheat and subcooling. If refrigerant is low, we find and repair the leak before recharging. Fixing the root cause is what keeps the system from freezing again a week later.</p><h2>Ductwork can cause freezing too</h2><p>Undersized or crushed return ducts reduce airflow across the coil. In homes where freezing keeps happening despite clean filters, ductwork is often the culprit.</p><h2>Thermostat settings that cause freezing</h2><p>Setting the thermostat extremely low on a hot day doesn&#x27;t cool the house faster; it just keeps the AC running longer, which can contribute to freezing if airflow is marginal. Set a reasonable temperature and let the system work. Using the Auto fan setting also helps the coil drain moisture properly.</p><h2>Night-time freezing</h2><p>Some systems freeze overnight when outdoor temperatures drop and the AC keeps running. If your AC is often frozen in the morning, a technician can check charge, airflow and controls.</p><h2>The bottom line</h2><p>A frozen air conditioner is a warning sign, not just an inconvenience. Thawing it gets you cooling again temporarily, but the underlying cause, whether it&#x27;s airflow, a dirty coil, a blower problem or a refrigerant leak, needs to be fixed to prevent repeat freezing and protect your compressor. Turn the system off, let it thaw, replace the filter and call us if it happens again. We&#x27;ll find the cause and fix it right.</p>
