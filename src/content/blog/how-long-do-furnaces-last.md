<h2>Typical furnace lifespan</h2><p>Most gas furnaces last roughly 15 to 20 years. Some run longer with good care, while others fail earlier because of poor installation, oversizing or neglect. Chicago&#x27;s long heating season means furnaces here run more hours per year than in milder climates.</p><h2>What shortens furnace life</h2><p>Dirty filters, restricted airflow, oversized equipment that short cycles, skipped maintenance and poor venting all add wear. Furnaces in damp basements can also suffer from corrosion.</p><h2>What extends furnace life</h2><p>Regular filter changes, a yearly professional tune-up, proper sizing and good ductwork help a furnace reach the long end of its expected life. Keeping the area around the furnace clean and dry helps too.</p><h2>Signs your furnace is nearing the end</h2><p>Frequent repairs, rising gas bills, uneven heating, more noise, a yellow burner flame, rust and corrosion or a furnace that&#x27;s simply 15 to 20 years old are all signs to start planning.</p><h2>Planning ahead</h2><p>Replacing a furnace on your schedule lets you compare options, consider high-efficiency models, check rebates and arrange financing, rather than accepting whatever is available during a January emergency.</p><h2>Repair or replace?</h2><p>If your furnace is older and needs a major repair, replacement often makes more sense. We&#x27;ll give you honest options for both.</p><h2>Furnace age and efficiency</h2><p>As furnaces age, efficiency slowly declines. Burners get dirty, heat exchangers collect deposits and motors work harder. A 20-year-old furnace that was 80 percent efficient when new may perform worse today. Modern high-efficiency furnaces at 95 percent and above can noticeably reduce gas bills in Chicago&#x27;s long heating season, which is one reason many homeowners choose to replace before a total failure.</p><h2>Furnace age and safety</h2><p>Older furnaces are more likely to develop cracked heat exchangers, worn gas valves and deteriorated venting. That&#x27;s why annual safety inspections and carbon monoxide testing become more important as a furnace gets older. Corroded flue pipes, in particular, are something we find often in older basements and replace whenever we see them.</p><h2>Parts availability</h2><p>As furnaces age, manufacturers stop making some parts. Repairs on very old furnaces can take longer while parts are located, and generic replacements may not perform as well. That&#x27;s another factor to consider when weighing repair versus replacement.</p><h2>Budgeting for replacement</h2><p>If your furnace is 12 to 15 years old, it&#x27;s a good time to start budgeting. Knowing your options ahead of time, including financing and possible rebates, means you won&#x27;t be forced into a rushed decision if the furnace fails in January.</p><h2>Extending the life of your current furnace</h2><p>Change filters regularly, keep the area around the furnace clean and dry, schedule yearly tune-ups and fix small problems early. These simple steps help a furnace reach the long end of its expected life.</p><h2>Comparing old and new furnaces</h2><p>A furnace from the 1990s or early 2000s likely has a single-stage burner, a basic blower and standard efficiency. Today&#x27;s furnaces offer two-stage or modulating burners, variable-speed blowers, better diagnostics and efficiency ratings of 95 percent and above. The difference in comfort, noise and gas use can be significant, especially in Chicago&#x27;s long heating season.</p><h2>When to get a second opinion</h2><p>If you&#x27;re told your furnace needs replacement, it&#x27;s reasonable to ask what failed and why, and to get a second opinion for major decisions. We&#x27;re happy to provide honest assessments.</p><h2>Warranty coverage</h2><p>Most new furnaces come with parts warranties, and many heat exchangers carry longer warranties, sometimes with registration required. Keeping records of maintenance helps if you ever need to use your warranty. Ask your installer to register your furnace and keep the paperwork with your home records.</p><h2>Getting the most from a new furnace</h2><p>When you do replace your furnace, proper sizing, good ductwork, a clean installation and yearly maintenance give your new system the best chance of a long, trouble-free life.</p><h2>Signs it&#x27;s time to start shopping</h2><p>If your furnace is past 15 years old, has needed more than one significant repair recently, struggles to keep up in cold weather or makes your gas bills climb year after year, it&#x27;s a good time to start gathering replacement options. Getting a free estimate doesn&#x27;t commit you to anything, but it gives you the information you need if your furnace fails unexpectedly.</p><h2>How we help</h2><p>We&#x27;ll give you an honest assessment of your current furnace, explain options at different efficiency levels and price points and help you check for rebates and financing.</p>
