<h2>Why winter air is so dry</h2><p>Cold outdoor air holds little moisture. When it leaks into your home and is heated, the relative humidity drops sharply.</p><h2>Signs your air is too dry</h2><p>Static shocks, dry skin, cracked lips, scratchy throats and gaps in wood floors are all common signs.</p><h2>Whole-home humidifiers</h2><p>A humidifier connected to your furnace adds moisture to the whole house automatically, without filling portable tanks.</p><h2>Don&#x27;t overdo it</h2><p>Too much humidity on very cold days causes condensation on windows. An automatic control adjusts for outdoor temperature.</p><h2>Measure your humidity</h2><p>An inexpensive hygrometer tells you your home&#x27;s humidity. Many smart thermostats display it too. Readings below 25 percent in winter are common in Chicago homes without humidifiers.</p><h2>Seal air leaks</h2><p>Air leaks bring in cold, dry outdoor air. Sealing them helps your home hold moisture and saves energy at the same time.</p><h2>Portable vs. whole-home</h2><p>Portable humidifiers help single rooms but need daily filling and cleaning. A whole-home system treats the entire house with minimal maintenance.</p><h2>Health and comfort effects</h2><p>Very dry air can dry out skin, eyes and nasal passages, aggravate allergies and make it harder to sleep. Many people notice fewer nosebleeds and less irritation after adding a humidifier.</p><h2>Protecting your home</h2><p>Wood floors, trim, cabinets, furniture and instruments shrink in dry air, which can cause gaps and cracks. Balanced humidity protects them.</p><h2>Choosing a humidifier type</h2><p>Bypass humidifiers are simple and affordable, fan-powered models add more output, and steam humidifiers provide the most moisture and precise control. We&#x27;ll recommend the right one for your home.</p><h2>Maintaining a whole-home humidifier</h2><p>Replace the water panel at the beginning of each heating season, make sure the water supply valve is open, check the drain line for clogs and set the damper to the winter position. If you have an automatic control, it will adjust humidity based on outdoor temperature.</p><h2>When to call a pro</h2><p>If your humidifier isn&#x27;t adding moisture, leaks or never seems to run, a technician can check the solenoid valve, control, wiring and water supply.</p><h2>Dry air and older Chicago homes</h2><p>Older homes with original windows, plaster walls and less insulation tend to be leakier, which means more cold, dry air enters and indoor humidity drops lower. That&#x27;s one reason dry air complaints are so common in Chicago bungalows and two-flats. Sealing leaks helps the home hold moisture and reduces the load on a humidifier.</p><h2>Humidity and wood floors</h2><p>Hardwood floors shrink in winter as they lose moisture, leaving visible gaps between boards. Trim, doors and furniture can crack. Keeping indoor humidity steady protects your woodwork and reduces seasonal gaps.</p><h2>Humidity and sleep</h2><p>Dry air can irritate your throat and sinuses at night, leading to poor sleep and morning congestion. Balanced humidity often helps people sleep more comfortably during long winters.</p><h2>Getting the setting right</h2><p>On milder winter days, 35 to 40 percent humidity is comfortable. On very cold days, lower humidity prevents condensation on windows. An automatic humidistat adjusts for you, which is why we recommend one with every whole-home humidifier we install.</p><h2>Static shocks and electronics</h2><p>Static shocks aren&#x27;t just annoying. Very dry air increases static buildup, which can be uncomfortable and, in rare cases, can affect sensitive electronics. Keeping humidity in a balanced range greatly reduces static. Many homeowners say the absence of static shocks is the first thing they notice after a whole-home humidifier is installed.</p><h2>Plants, pets and instruments</h2><p>Houseplants, pets and musical instruments also suffer in very dry air. Pianos and guitars can go out of tune or crack, plants dry out faster and pets may experience dry skin. Balanced humidity helps the whole household, not just the people.</p><h2>How humidity is measured</h2><p>Relative humidity tells you how much moisture the air holds compared to the most it could hold at that temperature. Cold air holds very little moisture, so when it&#x27;s heated, its relative humidity drops sharply. That&#x27;s why outdoor air that feels damp on a 20-degree day becomes extremely dry once it&#x27;s warmed to 70 degrees inside your home. A humidifier replaces that lost moisture, bringing relative humidity back into a comfortable range.</p><h2>Quick wins while you decide</h2><p>If you&#x27;re not ready for a whole-home humidifier yet, a few steps help: seal obvious drafts around windows and doors, keep lids on simmering pots while cooking, air-dry laundry indoors and use a portable humidifier in bedrooms, cleaning it often. These measures help a little, but in a Chicago winter, a properly controlled whole-home humidifier is by far the most effective and convenient solution for comfort throughout the house.</p>
