<h2>Your AC makes water</h2><p>As your AC cools, it pulls moisture out of the air. On humid Chicago days, it can remove many gallons of water, which drips into a pan and drains away.</p><h2>Clogged condensate drains</h2><p>Algae, dust and debris can clog the drain line or trap. Water backs up and overflows the pan. Clearing the drain and adding a cleanout makes future maintenance easier.</p><h2>Failed condensate pumps</h2><p>If your furnace is below a drain, a condensate pump lifts water out. Pumps fail, float switches stick and tubing clogs, leading to overflow.</p><h2>Frozen coil melting</h2><p>If the coil froze from low airflow or low refrigerant, it will drip heavily as it thaws.</p><h2>Attic air handlers</h2><p>Attic units should have a secondary drain pan and a float switch that shuts the AC off before water damages ceilings. We check these on every visit.</p><h2>Mini-split leaks</h2><p>Ductless units leak when their drain lines clog or when they aren&#x27;t level. A cleaning and drain repair usually fixes it.</p><h2>How much water an AC removes</h2><p>On a humid Chicago summer day, a central air conditioner can remove many gallons of water from your home&#x27;s air. All of that water has to go somewhere. When the drainage system works, you never notice. When it doesn&#x27;t, you&#x27;ll find puddles around the furnace, wet ceilings below attic units or water damage in finished basements.</p><h2>Algae and slime in drain lines</h2><p>Warm, moist drain lines are perfect for algae and slime growth. Over a season, this buildup can narrow or block the line. Flushing the drain during your spring tune-up and installing a cleanout makes future maintenance easier. Some homeowners also use drain line tablets recommended by their technician.</p><h2>Float switches protect your home</h2><p>A float switch in the drain pan or condensate line shuts the AC off if water backs up. If your AC suddenly stops and there&#x27;s water in the pan, the float switch likely did its job. Clearing the drain and resetting the switch restores operation.</p><h2>Damaged or rusted drain pans</h2><p>Older drain pans can rust through or crack, letting water leak even when the drain is clear. Replacing a damaged pan or adding a secondary pan protects your home.</p><h2>Water from a mini-split</h2><p>If a ductless indoor unit drips, its drain line may be clogged or the unit may not be level. A professional cleaning clears buildup inside the unit and restores proper drainage.</p><h2>Where the water usually shows up</h2><p>In basements, water from a central AC typically appears around the base of the furnace or near the condensate pump. In attic installations, the first sign may be a stain on the ceiling below. In closets, you may see water on the floor or baseboards. Wherever it appears, turn off the system and call for service to prevent damage.</p><h2>Preventing leaks</h2><p>Have the condensate drain flushed every spring, test the float switch, keep filters clean to prevent coil freezing and make sure condensate pumps are working. Attic systems should always have a secondary pan and safety switch.</p><h2>Water and mold</h2><p>Ongoing leaks can lead to mold growth in drywall, insulation and flooring. Fixing leaks quickly protects your home&#x27;s structure and indoor air quality.</p><h2>Condensate pumps explained</h2><p>If your furnace and AC coil sit below the nearest drain, a condensate pump collects water in a small reservoir and pumps it up and out through tubing when the float rises. Pumps can fail from worn motors, stuck floats, clogged check valves or tubing that kinks or clogs. Many pumps include a safety switch that can shut down the AC if the reservoir overfills. During tune-ups, we clean the reservoir and test the pump and safety switch so you don&#x27;t come home to a puddle.</p><h2>High-efficiency furnace condensate too</h2><p>In homes with high-efficiency furnaces, the same pump may also handle furnace condensate in winter, so a pump failure can cause problems year-round.</p><h2>The bottom line</h2><p>Water leaking from your air conditioner is almost always a drainage problem: a clogged line, a failed pump, a damaged pan or a frozen coil melting. It&#x27;s rarely complicated to fix, but it should be addressed quickly to prevent water damage and mold. Turn off the system, clean up the water and call us. We&#x27;ll clear the drain, repair the cause and add safeguards like float switches and secondary pans where needed.</p>
