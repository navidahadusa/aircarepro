<h2>Furnace problems we see in Chicago homes</h2>
<p>In bungalows, the furnace is usually in the basement with ductwork running up through the walls, and upstairs rooms are often the first to go cold. In condos and rehabs, furnaces are tucked into closets and attics where heat and poor airflow shorten part life. High-efficiency furnaces vent through PVC pipes that can ice up on sub-zero nights, and their condensate drains can freeze in unheated spaces. We see all of it every winter, and our vans carry the igniters, flame sensors, inducers, pressure switches and capacitors that fix most Chicago no-heat calls on the first visit.</p>

<h2>About homes in Chicago</h2>
<p>Chicago is a city of neighborhoods, and its homes are as varied as its streets. On the North Side you&#x27;ll find vintage greystones, brick two-flats and three-flats, courtyard buildings and modern condos. The West Side has classic workers&#x27; cottages, Victorian houses and greystones, while the Northwest Side is the heart of the bungalow belt. Each of these homes heats and cools in its own way, and an HVAC company that works here needs to understand all of them.</p><h3>Old, oversized boilers and furnaces</h3><p>Equipment installed decades ago was often sized generously, and since then many homes have added insulation and new windows. Oversized systems short cycle, waste gas and wear out faster. When it&#x27;s time to replace, we right-size the new equipment with a load calculation.</p><h3>Hot upstairs, cold basement</h3><p>In bungalows and two-story homes, upstairs rooms often overheat in summer and the lower level stays cool. Return air, duct design and zoning are usually the fix, not a bigger AC.</p>

<h2>Furnace Repair problems we fix in Chicago</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Igniter and flame sensor failures</strong></li><li><strong>Frozen or clogged condensate drains</strong></li><li><strong>Inducer motor and pressure switch faults</strong></li><li><strong>Blower motor and capacitor problems</strong></li><li><strong>Control board and limit switch issues</strong></li></ul><p>Most no-heat calls come down to a handful of causes: a failed igniter, a dirty flame sensor, a blocked condensate drain on a high-efficiency furnace, a pressure switch fault, a worn inducer motor or a control board issue. Our technicians read the furnace&#x27;s fault codes, test each component in the sequence of operation and check gas pressure and temperature rise, so we replace the part that actually failed instead of guessing. Before we leave, we run the furnace through full cycles and check for safe venting and carbon monoxide.</p>

<h2>Signs you need furnace repair</h2>
<ul><li>Furnace runs but blows cold air</li><li>Furnace won&#x27;t start or keeps shutting off (short cycling)</li><li>Banging, squealing or rattling noises</li><li>Blinking error light on the control board</li><li>Some rooms are much colder than others</li><li>Higher gas bills with no change in use</li></ul>
