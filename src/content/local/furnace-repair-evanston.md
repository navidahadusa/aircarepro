<h2>Furnace repair for Evanston's mix of homes</h2>
<p>Evanston&#x27;s forced-air homes range from older houses that were converted from radiators to newer homes with high-efficiency furnaces. Converted homes often have long duct runs and small returns, which makes furnaces overheat and trip their limit switches. Homes close to the lake face strong winter winds that push furnaces to run nearly nonstop during cold snaps. We diagnose the root cause, whether it&#x27;s airflow, ignition, venting or controls, so your Evanston furnace keeps running through the rest of the season.</p>

<h2>About homes in Evanston</h2>
<p>Evanston sits right on the lake just north of Chicago, and its housing reflects more than a century of growth. Large Victorian, Prairie-style and Colonial homes line the streets near the lakefront, brick two-flats and apartment buildings fill neighborhoods closer to downtown, and newer condos have gone up around the Metra and CTA stations. Many homes around Northwestern University and in south and central Evanston are heated by boilers and radiators, while others run forced-air furnaces and central AC.</p><h3>Aging boilers in large older homes</h3><p>Big older houses often have big boilers that were installed when the home had no insulation. We repair them carefully and, when it&#x27;s time, replace them with properly sized high-efficiency boilers.</p><h3>Uneven heat in three-story homes</h3><p>Third floors and finished attics are often the hardest rooms to heat and cool. Mini-splits, zoning and duct improvements solve it.</p>

<h2>Furnace Repair problems we fix in Evanston</h2>
<ul><li><strong>Igniter and flame sensor failures</strong></li><li><strong>Frozen or clogged condensate drains</strong></li><li><strong>Inducer motor and pressure switch faults</strong></li><li><strong>Blower motor and capacitor problems</strong></li><li><strong>Control board and limit switch issues</strong></li></ul><p>Most no-heat calls come down to a handful of causes: a failed igniter, a dirty flame sensor, a blocked condensate drain on a high-efficiency furnace, a pressure switch fault, a worn inducer motor or a control board issue. Our technicians read the furnace&#x27;s fault codes, test each component in the sequence of operation and check gas pressure and temperature rise, so we replace the part that actually failed instead of guessing. Before we leave, we run the furnace through full cycles and check for safe venting and carbon monoxide.</p>

<h2>Signs you need furnace repair</h2>
<ul><li>Furnace runs but blows cold air</li><li>Furnace won&#x27;t start or keeps shutting off (short cycling)</li><li>Banging, squealing or rattling noises</li><li>Blinking error light on the control board</li><li>Some rooms are much colder than others</li><li>Higher gas bills with no change in use</li></ul>
